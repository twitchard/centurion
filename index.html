        Centurion Chess

        A hundred games of chess— at once

        Steer a hundred Stockfish engines, one arrow at a time

        Rules of Engagement

        1. A hundred engines. You have a hundred chess engines (Stockfish, depth 5), each playing a game against your opponent's engine.
        2. One arrow per turn. Your arrow overrides the moves chosen by the engines — of either player — in games where it is a legal move.
        3. Arrows linger, but decay. An arrow sways up to eight games on the move after you place it, and half as many each turn after that. You can stack arrows to recharge them.
        4. Majority wins. Take the majority of the undrawn games and the match is yours.
        5. The playout. After 100 moves you place no more arrows. The engines play it out.
        The fine print
        Resolution order
        After each arrow, every game advances one half-move. Arrows act newest first: each pulls up to its current strength in randomly chosen games where it is a legal move, and a game moved by one arrow this turn is skipped by the rest. Every untouched game plays Stockfish's best move.
        Decay schedule
        A new arrow starts at strength 8 and halves each round: 8 when placed (and on the opponent's reply), 4 around your next arrow, then 2, then 1, then it disappears. Placing the same arrow again adds 8 and resets its decay.
        Reading the board
        All games appear stacked on one board. Your pieces are drawn white and your opponent's black — whichever chess color you actually play. The number next to a piece shows how many games have it on that square; numbers and arrows are tinted gold for player 1 and crimson for player 2. If you play black, your view is flipped and your arrow is read through that flip: drawing e2→e4 on your view means e7→e5 in the games.
        Scoring
        Checkmate scores a point for the side that delivered it. Draws score nothing, and finished games leave the board. The match ends early once a player's lead exceeds the number of games still running; if everything finishes level, the match is a draw.
        Who plays white
        One of you plays white in every game, the other black, chosen at random when the match starts. The white side places the first arrow: tap a start square, then an end square.
        Solo mode
        You place every arrow. After each one, both half-moves play out back to back — no opponent arrows, just you steering a hundred Stockfish games.
