            A hundred games of chess— at once
            
        
            Command a hundred imperfect engines with plain words
            
      
            
      
            
      
            
        
        
        
        
            
          
          
        
            
        
            
          
          
        
            
        
      
            
      
            
        
            Rules of Engagement
            
        
              
          
            1. A hundred games. You own one color in a hundred simultaneous chess games, all shown stacked on one board.
              2. 
          
            2. Your soldiers are ordinary. Unled, each game's engine deliberately plays a mediocre move — aiming about a pawn below best — and every game drifts its own way.
              3. 
          
            3. One order per turn. Type a command of up to 40 characters (“e4”, “all knights advance”, “take the queen”) and submit it. Literal moves like e4 or Nf3 compile directly; other phrases go through the language model.
              4. 
          
            4. Orders constrain, soldiers choose. In every game with a matching legal move, your soldier picks among the moves you allowed — same imperfect judgment, better options. No match, and it plays freely.
              5. 
          
            5. Majority wins. Checkmates score a point each; take the majority of the undrawn games and the match is yours. After 100 turns the soldiers play it out alone.
              6. 
        
            
        
            
          The fine print
          
            
            
            Resolution order
            
            
            Each turn resolves one half-move in every active game. Stockfish ranks every legal move; your order filters that list in each game where it matches; the soldier then samples a move aiming a set centipawn distance below the position's best. A soldier who can see a forced mate always takes it.
            
            
            Reading the board
            
            
            All games appear stacked on one board. Your pieces are drawn white and your opponent's black — whichever chess color you actually play. The number next to a piece shows how many games have it on that square. If you play black, your view is flipped. Literal moves like e5 or c5 use standard chess square names; natural-language orders count ranks from your own side.
            
            
            The compiler
            
            
            Your words are translated into a formal move predicate by a language model when you submit an order. The order log shows how many games followed each instruction.
            
            
            Scoring
            
            
            Checkmate scores a point for the side that delivered it. Draws score nothing, and finished games leave the board. The match ends early once a player's lead exceeds the number of games still running; if everything finishes level, the match is a draw.
            
            
            Who plays white
            
            
            One of you plays white in every game, the other black, chosen at random when the match starts. The white side issues the first order.
            
            
            Solo mode
            
            
            You command white every turn; black's soldiers play unled. Beat the drift.

      Connection log
      
              
        Multiplayer syncs match state through Firebase Realtime Database - no
        peer-to-peer connection is involved, so it works anywhere this page loads.
        The host creates the room and starts the match when the guest arrives; after
        that, each move is published to the room and mirrored on the other device.
        If nothing happens, check that both devices are online and using the same
        6-digit code.
