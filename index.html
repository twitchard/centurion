        Centurion Chess

        How to play
        • You and your opponent are playing 100 games of chess at once: one of you plays white in every game, the other black, chosen at random when the match starts. The white side places the first arrow.
        • All games appear stacked on one board. Your pieces are drawn white, your opponent's black — whichever chess color you actually play. The number next to a piece shows how many games have it on that square.
        • If you play black, your view is flipped so your pieces start nearest you, and your arrow is read through that flip: drawing e2→e4 on your view means e7→e5 in the games.
        • On your turn, place one arrow: tap its start square, then its end square. Arrows are permanent and shared — they can move your opponent's games too.
        • After each arrow, every game advances one half-move. Arrows act newest first: each pulls up to its current strength in games where it is a legal move; every other game plays Stockfish's best move. A new arrow starts at strength 8 and halves each round — 8 when placed (and on the opponent's reply), 4 around your next arrow, then 2, then 1, then it disappears. Placing the same arrow again adds 8 and resets its decay.
        • Checkmate scores a point for the player whose side delivered it. Draws score nothing. Finished games leave the board.
        • You win when your lead is bigger than the number of games still running. If everything finishes level, the match is a draw.
        • Solo mode: you place every arrow. After each one, both half-moves play out back to back — no opponent arrows, just you steering 100 Stockfish games.
